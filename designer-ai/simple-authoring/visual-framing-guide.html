2D DEPTH WITHOUT FAKE 3D

Parallax

Foreground crosses faster, midground slower, background slowest. The audience reads depth even though every asset is a 2D layer.

Relative scale

A foreground fighter can be 0.35 frame width while a distant fleet is 0.04–0.08. That is depth by composition, not 3D.

Screen-space arc

An orbit-like move may be an X/Y ellipse while the same legal sprite view is preserved. Never imply unseen geometry.

IF A SHOT ONLY WORKS AFTER INVENTING A NEW VIEW ANGLE, IT IS NOT A LEGAL SHOT.

DEFAULT RHYTHM FOR OUR SPACE-ARCADE FILMS

Environment

Use a lot.
Wide city, battlefield, hangar, fleet, planet, corridor. These shots explain scale and geography.

Action

Medium-wide and wide. Show origin, target, motion and consequence in one readable plane when possible.

Dialogue

Medium by default. Close only when emotional importance justifies losing environmental context.

Detail

Radar alert, weapon arm, hand on switch, damaged engine, one reaction. Short and purposeful.

ANTI-PATTERN

Do not make every panel a portrait. If the viewer cannot tell where the character is, what surrounds them, what is approaching or how large the threat is, the storyboard has become a collection of passport photos with explosions.

WIDE IS NORMAL. CLOSE IS EARNED.

THE NAME OF A MOVE IS NOT ENOUGH
SETUP → VISUAL TRANSITION → PAYOFF. For every major move, DEVORA must be able to point to a visible difference between start and result. If Flyby, Approach, Intercept or Formation Break is only a decorative label over a generic move, the motion is semantically flat.
GOOD 2D MOVES

Flyby

Enter off-screen → cross one readable vector → optional track → exit. Facing/view remains legal.

Approach

Subject enters small → grows gradually → lands at a screen mark. No perspective rotation.

Intercept

Two screen vectors converge. Preserve who is moving which direction.

Pullback reveal

Hero becomes smaller while more environment/threat becomes visible.

Pass camera

Subject enters large near an edge, crosses, briefly crops, exits.

Formation break

Sprites share a direction, then diverge in X/Y after the formation is established.

CONTINUITY

Preserve direction

A hero travelling left-to-right should not suddenly appear travelling right-to-left unless the reversal is visibly shown.

Preserve subject

Camera target must be visible, entering or revealing during the shot. Never track an invisible entity just because its ID exists somewhere.

MOTION MUST EXPLAIN STORY. DECORATIVE MOVEMENT IS NOT CINEMATOGRAPHY.

A · IDENTITY IS NOT PRESENTATION

Cast semantics

identityHandle = who/what this entity actually is.

presentationHandle = optional preferred way to show that same identity.

sameIdentityAs = explicit declaration that two cast entries intentionally represent the same identity.

Stable identity example

GREEN PILOT stays GREEN PILOT across shots. A same-identity portrait/body presentation may change. The narrative identity may not silently become RED PILOT, another pilot, a bus or a similar-looking asset to satisfy resolution.

NEVER SUBSTITUTE ONE ACTOR IDENTITY JUST BECAUSE ANOTHER ASSET LOOKS SIMILAR OR BELONGS TO THE SAME FAMILY.
B · DIALOGUE IS STILL A FILM SHOT

DEVORA authors

speaker · listener · text · mood/performance intent · visible world evidence · camera purpose · composition.

Unity resolves exact Dialogue Stage mechanics. Do not hand-author V5 Dialogue Stage internals in Simple V1.

ACTIVE SPEAKER · NEAR
LISTENER · FAR
DIALOGUE MUST NOT ERASE THE THING THE DIALOGUE IS ABOUT.
World-evidence dialogue: If the Captain says “The blockade is closing,” and the blockade matters physically, keep blockade evidence visible through a world shot, monitor, radio/action communication, hero-in-environment or equivalent supported composition. Do not automatically replace the world with two giant portraits. When speaker changes, NEAR/FAR emphasis reverses; identity does not.
C · CINEMATIC MOVE IS NOT JUST A LABEL

A major move should normally contain 2–4 meaningful visual states. If one Simple V1 beat cannot faithfully express all stages, split the move into multiple beats using the same semantic identity.
